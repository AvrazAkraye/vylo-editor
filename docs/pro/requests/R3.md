# R3 (interface review): requests to the logic owners

Things the interface review found that live in files it does not own. Each says what a person sees, where the
cause is, and a way to fix it. The full review is `docs/pro/review-interface.md`.

## 1. "+ Scene" on a template graphic takes away its words form (motionscene.ts, motionedit.ts) — important

**What a person sees.** Home, pick Big title, then press the quiet "+ Scene" above the timeline. The graphic
grows by an empty three-second scene, and Design's first section, *Words on screen*, which held the template's
three fields a moment ago, now says "This graphic has no template words to change here. Change its words in
Layers." The one thing the shortest path is about (change the words) has moved to another tab, because of a
button that looked like it only adds time. Seen in the off-screen WebKit harness (picture `v-add` in the review's
notes), in all four languages.

**Why.** `addScene` (and `moveScene`) move layers in time, and the file's rule is that such edits drop the template
link (`detach`, motionscene.ts ~506), because `motionedit.ts`'s `rebuild` builds the template for the whole
graphic's length: kept, the next word typed would rebuild the title across every scene and undo the scene.

**A way to keep it.** Let the template own a span, not the whole graphic:

- `RecipeRef` (motionrecipe.ts / motiontypes.ts) gains an optional `until?: number`: the template's layers end there.
  Absent means the whole graphic, as today, so nothing stored changes.
- `addScene` at or after the end of the template's span keeps `recipe` and sets `until` to the old end (the cut);
  layers it creates or moves after the cut are the person's. Adding a scene *inside* the template's span (before
  its end) still detaches, as today.
- `rebuild` builds the template for `until` seconds (not `seconds`), keeps every layer whose id the fresh build
  does not make (the added scenes' layers) exactly where it is, and keeps `seconds`, `scenes` and `sound` as they
  are. `setSeconds` on such a graphic moves `until` only when the new length cuts into it.
- `splitSceneAt` and `setTransition` already keep the link; `renameScene` too.

The test is the shortest path itself: build Big title, `addScene(m, 1)`, `setFields(m, { title: 'New' })` — the
title changes, the second scene's layers and the scenes are unchanged, and `readMotion` is a fixed point. Until
then, the interface could at least warn on the button (a tooltip "Adds a scene; the words are then changed in
Layers"), but a warning is a decision the contract says the short path should not have.

## 2. The check's `empty-frame` repair and scenes (motioncheck.ts) — small

Not seen in the pictures (a graphic with a new, empty scene said "Looks good", because its background runs on
through it), but the W2-1 note stands: a scene with nothing but a background is legitimately empty while
somebody is building it, and the "Trim the end" repair of an empty stretch at the end would delete that scene.
`empty-frame` could skip a stretch that is a whole scene (`doc.scenes`), or offer no repair for it.

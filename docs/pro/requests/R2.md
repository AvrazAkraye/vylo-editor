# Requests from review R2 (export, sound and sync)

These are problems the review found in files outside R2's lane, with the evidence and a proposed fix for each.
R2's own fixes are in `docs/pro/review-export.md`. The first request is about sync and should be settled before
release. The second matters on a full disk. The rest are smaller.

## 1. `motionsound.ts` cue derivation (package 04, with 05): sounds play for exits nobody sees

When a scene arrives with any transition except a cut, the scene before it **holds still** from its hold
moment until the cut (`sceneHold`, docs/pro/scenes.md: "the transition is the exit"). `soundCues` does not
know this. It still gives each of those layers its exit sound, so a whoosh plays over a still picture, about
half a second before the transition actually moves.

Measured on the four-scene sample (`vylo-pro-samples/mp4/04-scenes-push-iris-flash.mp4`; the script is
`.test-build/r2/holdcheck.mjs`):

| scene | holds still | until the cut | cues in that still stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 → push | 2.400 s | 3 s | whoosh @2.55 (reversed, gain 0.55) |
| 2 → iris | 5.550 s | 6 s | whoosh @5.55 (reversed, 0.55) |
| 3 → flash | 8.400 s | 9 s | whoosh @8.40 (0.35), whoosh @8.55 (0.36) |

The same thing happens at every cut of the 30-second, ten-scene sample.

**Proposed fix.** In `allCues`, when `doc.scenes` has a scene `i + 1` that arrives with any transition other
than `cut`, drop every cue whose time falls in `[sceneHold(doc, i), scene i's end)`. Those cues belong to
motion that is never drawn: exits, and the entrances of layers that start after the hold, which scenes.md
says are never seen. `motionscene.ts` does not import `motionread.ts` or `motiondraw.ts`, so importing
`sceneHold` keeps the import rule. Package 04 could also give the transition a sound of its own at the cut,
for example a whoosh for push, slide, whip and zoom, a swell for fade and iris, and a short riser into a
flash. That is a design choice, so R2 has not made it.

## 2. `src-tauri/src/video.rs` `export_write_video` (Video's file): a full disk

The film is already finished in memory when Rust is asked to write it, so a full disk only shows up at write
time.

- **Save as…, over an existing file:** `fs::write(&p, bytes)` truncates the old file before it writes. If the
  write fails with `ENOSPC` part-way, the old file is gone and a partial MP4 is left at the path. Its index
  comes first, so it opens as a file but its picture is cut short. The panel shows an error, but the old file
  cannot be recovered. **Proposed:** write to a sibling temporary name in the same folder (`create_new`), call
  `sync_all`, then `rename` it over the target. On any error, remove the temporary file.
- **Download (`write_new`):** a failed `write_all` already removes the partial file. The gaps are that `File`'s
  drop ignores close errors and nothing calls `sync_all`. On network volumes (SMB, NFS) and some FUSE file
  systems, `ENOSPC` or `EIO` only arrives at close or fsync, so the app reports success for a truncated file.
  **Proposed:** call `f.sync_all()` before returning `Ok`, and remove the file if it fails.
- **The sentence** (`MotionExport.tsx`, `sentence()`): a full disk falls through to `explain()`, which shows
  the OS's words ("No space left on device (os error 28)"). **Proposed:** add a case for os error 28 that says
  "The disk is full. Free some space, or save somewhere else." in four languages.

**For the owner, not code:** during this review the Mac's system volume reached **99% (148 MB free)**. The
Downloads folder, where Download saves, is on that volume. About 5 GB of it was in
`kiro-cli-download*` folders in the user temp folder (`/var/folders/…/T/`). Those folders belong to another
app, so R2 did not touch them. An export of up to about 300 MB (30 s of 4K at the top quality) could fail
there.

## 3. `motionplay.ts` (the clock): small sync costs at play, seek and loop

- The first tick after `play()` or `seek()` adds no time (`stamp = 0`), so the picture runs **one frame
  behind the sound** after every play and seek. Measured in WebKit: 15 to 18 ms at 60 Hz, and it would be
  33 ms at 30 Hz. That is under BT.1359's 45 ms threshold, but it is easy to avoid: set `stamp` to
  `performance.now()` at play and seek instead of 0.
- The playhead counts at most 0.25 s of any gap between frames. A main-thread stall longer than that loses
  time against the sound. The preview now catches this (R2 fix: it restarts the sound when the sound runs
  45 ms ahead). The lasting fix is a time source: while sound plays, the playhead takes its time from the
  audio clock (for example `setTimeSource(() => position())` from `motionsoundplay.ts`). Then a stall cannot
  separate the two at all.
- Looping: when `t ≥ lastFrame`, `t %= seconds` leaves `t` in `[lastFrame, seconds)` for one frame, a moment
  past the last frame. The sound refuses to start that close to its end, so nothing is lost, but the playhead
  shows a time the film does not have.

## 4. `motionsound.ts` design numbers (package 04): two settings for the owner's ears

These are not bugs. They are numbers that someone should hear before release.

- **Sparse effects stay below −16 LUFS.** With Effects only, at level 60%, six templates finish 1.3 to 2.9 LU
  under target. ffmpeg's meter on the exported films reads loop-bg −17.8, lt-pill −18.0, lt-neon −18.9,
  ui-scribble −17.3, ui-chat −17.6 and film-look −17.9 LUFS. The cause is `BOOST_FX_DB = 10`: the finishing
  pass raises sparse effects by at most 10 dB. BS.1770's gate already ignores the silence between them. The six are 1.3
  to 2.9 dB short, so by that arithmetic a cap of about 13 dB would bring all six to −16. That has not been
  measured. The limiter still holds the peaks either way.
- **Panning.** A cue is panned by where its layer sits, up to ±0.7. A graphic whose only moving layer is at
  one side ends up lopsided: the 24 fps lower-third sample measures **4.6 dB** louder on the left over the whole
  film, and a single cue at the edge is 12.4 dB apart between channels. On earbuds, a lower third's whoosh
  is almost entirely in one ear. Consider `PAN_WIDTH` 0.4 to 0.5.

## 5. `videosynth.ts` (Video's composer, read-only here): drum hits with a one-sample attack

In the counter sample's music (`02-counter-both`, 1.653 s), a drum onset jumps by 0.30 of full scale in one
sample. The jump is already in the bed before encoding, so it is not an encoding artifact. It is a percussive
attack, and some ears will hear it as a click. If that is not intended, a 1 to 2 ms attack ramp on the
percussion voices would remove it. This is Video's file, so it is only noted here.

## The model, and why

A scene is a stretch of time (`{ id, name, start, end, transition? }`, back to back, the transition saying how
the scene *arrives*). A layer belongs to the scenes it is on screen during; there is no `scene` field. That keeps
the flat layers, every existing edit, the timeline, the chat and the model's vocabulary unchanged, and a document
can never name a scene that is not there. The cost: a layer running across a cut is in both pictures, so a push
moves it out and back in, as it moves a whole picture. When a scene arrives with a transition, the scene before
it holds still from its hold moment (the last instant before any of its layers begins to leave) until the cut:
**the transition is the exit**, HyperFrames' rule. A cut leaves everything exactly as designed. Full reasoning in
`docs/pro/scenes.md`.

## Tests and timings

`npm test` passes (about 35 s for the whole chain on this machine); `test/pro-scenes.test.mjs` has 151 checks and
runs in about 2.3 s. `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` pass.

- **No scenes, nothing changes.** In the suite: the eighteen templates, nine moments each, record identical draw
  calls with `scenes` absent, empty, one scene, or cuts only; a graphic with a transition paints its own frame at
  every moment outside the transition and its hold. Outside the suite, once: Phase 0's own `motiondraw.ts`
  (commit c921d23) bundled beside this one, 2,592 frames (every template, shape and language, nine moments)
  compared call for call: **0 differ** (the comparison does see a difference at a transition frame, so it is not
  vacuous).
- **Transitions:** at 0 only the old picture is drawn and at 1 only the new, for all 13 kinds, 4 directions, both
  reading directions; every curve offered rises monotonically from exactly 0 to 1 (the five left out are shown to
  turn back); nine kinds' own measure of "how much of the new scene" only grows; push, slide, whip, up/down, clock,
  blinds and light leak mirror in Arabic; the same moment and seed draw the same calls; every frame is clean on the
  recording canvas (finite numbers, balanced saves, valid composite operations, no `filter`).
- **The frame at a cut:** the hold moment, the held frame equal to the graphic at that moment call for call, the
  old picture at the cut and the new one mid-way equal to the graphic at their moments, two canvases made once and
  reused, motion blur over a transition, the fall-back to a cut with no offscreen canvas.
- **Reading:** limits, ordering, ids, names, aliases, and about 3,600 hostile and random inputs (throwing Proxies
  and getters, cycles, a sparse array of 2³²−1, `__proto__`, five thousand scenes): no throw, always valid, a fixed
  point. `readMotion` keeps scenes and stays a fixed point.
- **Edits:** every rule in the table in `scenes.md`, then about 1,200 random edits on the templates: no throw,
  valid scenes, `readMotion` reads each result back unchanged, clean frames round every cut.
- **Cost:** a transition frame is 2.01x a normal frame's time and 2.06x its canvas calls (recording canvas, 480 x
  270); 2.04x in WKWebView at 960 x 540.
- **House rules:** `motionscene.ts` reaches neither `motiondraw.ts` nor `motionread.ts` at run time (checked by
  walking the import graph); nothing sets `ctx.filter` or uses an unseeded random.
- **The strip** is bundled and rendered with `react-dom/server`: one scene is a lone quiet "+ Scene"; three scenes
  are three chips and two transition chips, one tab stop, the current scene marked, the track `dir="ltr"`; the
  strip's CSS has no physical left or right.

**Checked in WKWebView** (macOS 26.2, a throwaway off-screen harness with a non-persistent store; not part of the
suite): no seams at any progress for any kind; exact fades; transparency handled; a contact sheet of all thirteen
through `paint` and screenshots of the strip in English (light), Arabic (dark) and Sorani with a menu open looked
right. This found and fixed three real problems (see `scenes.md`, "Checked in the app's own engine") and one in the
strip: a menu inside the left-to-right track was placed by its own direction in WebKit and ran off-screen in Arabic,
so the menu box now keeps the track's direction and only its contents take the interface's.

## Deviations from the brief

- **Props.** `{ t: T; doc; onEdit: OnEdit; onSeek? }`, not `{ doc; t: number; onChange(next); onSeek(t) }`: `t`
  is the translator in every Motion component, and edits go through `onEdit(change, key)` so history can merge a
  drag. The playhead comes from `motionplay.ts`, as the timeline's does.
- **The "+ Scene" behaviour** grows the graphic (3 s, or what is left under 30 s) rather than cutting the current
  scene; "Split here" is the cut. With one scene the strip shows only "+ Scene", as the brief says, so a person
  splits a one-scene graphic by adding a scene first (or the chat can split it).
- **`removeScene` joins** a scene to its neighbour and keeps its layers (the brief: "merge into its neighbour"); the
  menu says "Join with the scene before/after" so nobody expects content to vanish.
- **Glitch** is the approximation the brief allows (slices that wrap round, red and cyan copies with `lighter`).

## Open problems

- **Stage outline and hit-testing** use live poses, so during a hold or a transition a leaving layer's outline can
  be off from the frozen picture (request 6).
- **A layer that starts after its scene's hold moment** is never seen when the next scene arrives with a
  transition. Follows from the rule; a check tip could flag it.
- **Template rebuilds drop scenes**, and `setSeconds` leaves them stale until the graphic is read again (requests 2
  and 3). `paint` copes (it tidies the list every frame), so nothing draws wrong, but the strip shows the stale list
  until then.
- **A new layer** from "Add layer" spans the whole graphic, so in a scened graphic it runs through every scene
  (request 4).
- **The glitch's colour fringes** brighten dark areas a little at the peak (`lighter`); accepted as the look.
- **Translations** for Sorani and Badini are best effort (review-needed.md); the Arabic is mine and should be read
  by a native speaker too.
- **Not verified:** keyboard and screen-reader behaviour of the strip in the real app (only rendered statically and
  in screenshots), WebView2 on Windows (the composite operations used are standard and Chromium follows the spec,
  but it was not run), and performance on a long 4K export with motion blur (each blur sub-frame of a transition
  paints two pictures).
